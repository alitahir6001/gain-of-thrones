# Heavy Metal/Punk

> Preset: `heavy-guitar` (`src/gain_of_thrones/presets/heavy_guitar.py`). Research notes: [heavy-metal-punk/](heavy-metal-punk/).

## Rebalance the stems, then stop squeezing

Professional remastering practice, the academic literature and the punk/metal mixing canon all point the same way for this EP: treat the CD as a finished, already-limited master, fix tonal balance and masking with small, wide moves, and spend loudness carefully, because the source has no headroom left to give. The client's "cloudy" guitars, reverby and buried vocal, and vanishing cymbals map onto well-documented frequency conflicts: guitar mud at 200 to 400 Hz, guitar/vocal masking at 2.4 to 3.8 kHz, and guitar/cymbal masking at 3 to 6 kHz. Mixing references give concrete starting numbers for all three ([SOS, Mixing Metal](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/mixing-metal); [SOS Mix Rescue](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/mix-rescue-generals-majors)). Demucs stems make those fixes possible from a stereo master, but htdemucs_ft's weakest stem is exactly the one holding the guitars ("other", 6.32 dB SDR against 9.0 dB overall), and remixing studies find that even a 6 dB vocal boost via separation produces "small but perceptible" degradation ([Rouard et al., ICASSP 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08553); [Wierstorf et al., AES 143](https://aes2.org/publications/elibrary-page/?id=19277)). So per-stem gain should stay within about plus or minus 2 dB and per-stem EQ within about plus or minus 3 dB at wide Q, with the stem-sum residual added back at unity. The one place the client's preference conflicts with the evidence is loudness: their favorite version (-9.5 LUFS integrated, -1.2 dBTP) has a peak-to-loudness ratio (PLR) of 8.3 dB, roughly 3 dB tighter than the source CD's ~11 dB. That extra limiting is a plausible contributor to the cymbal complaint, and "louder sounds better" bias in unmatched A/B tests is well documented. The -1.2 dBTP ceiling is fine. The -9.5 LUFS figure deserves one loudness-matched blind check before it is locked in.

## A hot reissue CD is a repair job, not a fresh master

Every engineer quoted in the research treats source generation as the first variable. Steve Hoffman refused a label's "EQ dub" and asked for the untouched mixdown or a flat 1:1 analog safety copy with no EQ, compression or limiting ([Headphonesty, Aug 2025](https://www.headphonesty.com/2025/08/mastering-legend-refused-touch-classic-album/)). Bob Ludwig says "there is no rule" that a remaster beats the original: the result depends on who did it and which tapes they had ([Digital Bits / MusicTAP](https://archive.thedigitalbits.com/articles/musictap/bobludwig.html)). A mastered CD is, in Hoffman's terms, an EQ dub plus a limiter. Clipping printed into a master can survive even a full multitrack remix: reviewers of the 2013 Rush *Vapor Trails* remix said some clipping "can likely never be fixed" ([Popdose](https://popdose.com/popdose-roundtable-rush-vapor-trails-remixed/)). Dave Gardner's rule from the Bad Brains reissues is the right operating principle here: "Before you can even make it better, you have to really make sure you're not making it worse" ([The Pitch](https://www.thepitchkc.com/engineer-dave-gardner-on-making-bad-brains-sound-better-than-ever-before/)).

The genre history also suggests this CD is not the most dynamic version of the record that ever existed. **Original 1980s CDs in this genre were fairly dynamic**: Black Flag's *Slip It In* SST CD (1984) logs **DR13** ([DR Database](https://dr.loudness-war.info/album/view/17829)), and the 1986 *Master of Puppets* sat at roughly DR12 before George Marino's 1995 remaster cut it to **DR8** ([Angry Metal Guy](https://www.angrymetalguy.com/angry-metal-fi-re-mastering-remasters/)). The 2005 Dead Kennedys *Fresh Fruit* reissue reads **DR7** ([DR Database](https://dr.loudness-war.info/album/view/206375)). Fan and critic reception consistently rewards reissues that restore dynamics, such as Weinberg's 2016 Metallica remasters and Earache's "Full Dynamic Range" line, and punishes louder ones ([Angry Metal Guy](https://www.angrymetalguy.com/angry-metal-fi-re-mastering-remasters/)). *Inference:* an EP at -10 to -12 LUFS with an LRA of 1 to 3 LU is more likely a 1990s-2000s reissue than a first-issue CD. If an earlier CD or vinyl pressing can be found, it could beat any amount of processing on this one. Running the TT DR offline meter on the rip would give a real baseline; the DR method takes peak minus the mean of the loudest 20% of 3-second RMS windows ([Stereophile](https://stereophile.com/content/dynamic-range-database)).

What can be repaired from a hot CD is narrower than what tools promise. iZotope RX De-clip interpolates over flat-topped samples above a threshold set "just below" the clipping. Its docs warn that restored peaks exceed 0 dBFS and survive only in 32-bit float, and iZotope states the tool is "not intended for brickwall-limited audio" because limiting does not create squared-off waveforms ([RX De-clip docs](https://help.izotope.com/docs/rx-plug-in-pack/De-clip.html); [iZotope distortion guide](https://www.izotope.com/en/learn/repairing-a-distorted-audio-track)). For limiting, the relevant tools are ML de-limiters. Jeon and Lee's open-source De-limiter (WASPAA 2023) predicts per-sample gains that invert limiting and recovers the unlimited mix at about **24 dB SI-SDR** on its synthetic test set ([arXiv 2308.01187](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.01187)). Ozone 12's Unlimiter builds on that work and is pitched for "remastering older recordings", with the caveat that "severely brick-walled or distorted sources were not a focus" ([iZotope, Inside Ozone 12](https://www.izotope.com/en/learn/inside-ozone-12)). *Inference:* both were trained on modern plug-in limiting, so their behavior on 1980s analog-era limiting and on intentionally distorted guitars is unknown. A de-clipper can mistake guitar distortion for clipping, so only de-clip where the sample histogram shows true flat-topping, and audition any de-limiting at low Amount settings. Broadband noise reduction should default to off: a dense, loud punk master hides tape hiss, and NR would smear cymbals and guitar fizz first. Hum gets a targeted notch only if it is measurable in gaps (inference, consistent with Hoffman's "limited restoration... without altering the rest of the sound" ([Tape Op #85](https://tapeop.com/interviews/85/steve-hoffman))).

## Separation quality sets a hard ceiling on per-stem moves

The benchmark numbers explain where artifacts will show up. On MUSDB18-HQ, **htdemucs_ft scores 9.00 dB mean SDR: drums 10.08, bass 10.39, vocals 9.20, and "other" only 6.32** ([Rouard et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08553)). On professionally produced, non-normalized Sony test material in the MDX'21 challenge, scores dropped further (the Hybrid Demucs winner had "other" at 5.19 dB), and the organizers flagged a generalization gap ([Mitsufuji et al., Frontiers 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/frsip.2021.808395)). In expert listening tests the predecessor Hybrid Demucs scored **2.83/5 for quality and 3.04/5 for absence of contamination** ([Défossez, MDX 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.03600v3)). Limiting is itself documented as a separate degradation domain (the musdb-XL dataset exists to study it) ([Jeon et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.01187v2)). *Inference:* a limited, distorted 1980s master is out-of-domain for Demucs, so expect guitar stems carrying vocal and cymbal bleed, and palm-muted low guitars leaking into bass and drums.

The client's measured stem-sum residual of -20 to -22 dB needs careful interpretation. *Inference:* it measures mixture consistency (how well the four stems sum back to the input), not separation cleanliness. Bleed that sits in the wrong stem still sums back correctly, so a small residual does not mean clean stems. A 2025 paper proposing remix-consistency metrics treats summing back to the mixture as a desirable property, not a guarantee of quality ([arXiv 2504.17782](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.17782)). The residual is still about 1% of the signal energy and plausibly holds transients, cymbal top end and distortion products the model did not assign. *Inference, standard remix-from-separation practice but not perceptually evaluated in any paper found:* compute the residual as the input minus the sum of the stems, and add it back at unity gain into the mix bus before bus processing. With all stem processing bypassed, the sum then nulls against the source, and the cymbal and transient content that would otherwise vanish is preserved.

The remixing literature is the evidence for keeping moves modest. Wierstorf et al. found separation-based vocal boosts of up to 6 dB came "at the cost of introducing a small but perceptible degradation" ([AES 143](https://aes2.org/publications/elibrary-page/?id=19277)). Yang et al. showed that cascaded separate-then-remix pipelines produce audible artifacts, which are smallest when the remix stays close to the original balance ([arXiv 2107.13634](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.13634)). Separation metrics are "far from perfect" predictors of what listeners hear, so the ear is the final check ([Ward et al., ICASSP 2018](https://hagenw.github.io/pubs/ward_et_al-2018-predicting_the_perception_of_singing_voice_separation.pdf)). Mastering EQ convention independently arrives at a similar limit: boosts and cuts within about 3 dB at wide Q, with one vendor's "Master Mode" capping moves at 4 dB and Q 1.5 ([Mastering The Mix](https://www.masteringthemix.com/blogs/learn/how-to-eq-during-mastering)). *Inference from these results:* keep stem gain changes within plus or minus 2 dB, EQ within plus or minus 3 dB at Q of 1.5 or lower, solo stems only for diagnosis, and judge every move in the full sum. Avoid gating or downward expansion on "other". Gating a separated stem gates the bleed and the model's errors together, which exposes "musical noise" and pumping (inference from the academic notes). There is also an identity argument. The contested 2022 Chris Lord-Alge remix of *Fresh Fruit* shows that rebalancing, unlike remastering, changes a record's identity ([Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/dead-kennedys-fresh-fruit-for-rotting-vegetables-reissue-1378541/)). Stem work is a light remix, and small moves keep it sounding like the same record.

On the Demucs side, the README notes that `--shifts` averages predictions over randomly shifted inputs (N times slower), overlap defaults to 0.25, and htdemucs_ft is about 4x slower than htdemucs "but might be a bit better" ([GitHub facebookresearch/demucs](https://github.com/facebookresearch/demucs)). *Inference:* use a few shifts for the final render. Because the source peaks above 0 dBTP, either lower the input by 1 to 2 dB in float before separation or make sure stems are written in float, so the stems themselves do not clip. The research did not verify which output flags the current Demucs build exposes, so check `demucs --help`.

Three warnings apply to this table. First, these are mix-stage numbers for isolated tracks. On a summed drum stem, a kick move also hits snare and cymbal bleed, so the same EQ point should be cut or boosted less than on a close mic (inference). Second, mix-stage dynamics such as Mynett's 8:1 to 10:1 bass compression or Senior's 3:1 drum bus at 4 to 5 dB of gain reduction were written for raw tracks ([SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/mixing-metal); [Mix Rescue](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/mix-rescue-generals-majors)). Stems carved out of a limited master have already been squeezed, so *inference:* stem compression, if used at all, should stay near 2:1 with 20 to 30 ms attack and 1 to 2 dB of gain reduction. Third, the cymbal complaint probably has two causes. One is spectral masking: guitar energy at 2 to 6 kHz grows more prominent at high volume because hearing sensitivity peaks near 3 to 4 kHz (general psychoacoustics, not sourced in these notes). The other is dynamic: a limiter driven by a full-band guitar wall pulls cymbal decays down (inference). EQ fixes the first. Only less limiting fixes the second.

Mike Senior's hard-rock mix and Angry Metal Guy's critique of Megadeth's *Countdown to Extinction* (originally "guitars too far back and cymbals too far forward") both show that guitar/cymbal balance is usually a mix-level decision ([Angry Metal Guy](https://www.angrymetalguy.com/angry-metal-fi-re-mastering-remasters/)). That is exactly why stems help here, and also why the moves should stay within the separation ceiling above.

## The client's -9.5 LUFS costs about 3 dB of peak headroom

Mastering-stage dynamics in the sources are gentle. Bus compression typically runs at **1.5:1 to 2:1 with under about 3 dB of gain reduction**, a moderately slow attack and a fast or auto release ([Mastering The Mix](https://www.masteringthemix.com/blogs/learn/how-to-eq-during-mastering); [Bobby Owsinski](https://bobbyowsinskiblog.com/self-mastering-tips/)). One vendor's starting point is **about 40 ms attack, about 150 ms release, soft knee** ([Sage Audio](https://www.sageaudio.com/articles/mastering-compression-settings)). Mynett caps a metal master bus at **2:1 and 3 to 4 dB of gain reduction** ([SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/mixing-metal)). Bob Katz warns that "1 dB on an entire mix with the wrong unit can be severely degrading", that pumping depends on attack and release relative to tempo, and that he is "not a fan of multiband compression" ([Digido](https://www.digido.com/?p=1509)). For the limiter, Katz says "1 to 3 dB or so gain reduction can be very invisible, but invisible is not the same as natural" ([Digido](https://www.digido.com/?p=1509)). FabFilter documents that fast limiting and clipping create inter-sample peaks, and that 4x oversampling with true-peak mode holds them within about 0.1 dB ([FabFilter Pro-L 2 help](https://www.fabfilter.com/help/pro-l/using/truepeaklimiting)). The compressor goes before the limiter, and dither, if any, comes once at the very end ([MusicRadar](https://musicradar.com/news/tech/master-bus-compression-explained-by-a-mastering-engineer-629292); [iZotope](https://www.izotope.com/en/learn/what-is-dithering-in-audio.html)).

For loudness targets, Ian Shepherd's guideline is to keep the loudest sections no louder than **-10 LUFS short-term with true peaks no higher than -1 dBTP**, which usually lands **-11 to -14 LUFS integrated** ([Production Advice](https://productionadvice.co.uk/how-loud/)). Spotify normalizes to -14 LUFS (Loud mode -11), and asks for **-2 dBTP on masters louder than -14 LUFS** to avoid lossy-codec distortion ([Spotify for Artists](https://support.spotify.com/us/artists/article/loudness-normalization/)). The arithmetic on the client's numbers, which is calculation rather than measurement, is the following. The source CD's PLR is +0.2 to +0.3 dBTP minus -10 to -12 LUFS, or **10.2 to 12.3 dB**. The client's preferred version has a PLR of -1.2 minus -9.5, or **8.3 dB**. Reaching it means taking roughly 2 to 4 dB more peak-to-loudness range out of a master that was already limited, which puts the remaster's own limiter at or beyond the top of Katz's "invisible" 1 to 3 dB. With an LRA of 1 to 3 LU, short-term loudness at the loudest moments likely sits a dB or two above -9.5 LUFS, past Shepherd's -10 LUFS short-term guideline (inference).

The perception research gives the counter-argument fairly. Under loudness-matched conditions, Hjortkjær and Walther-Hansen found no reliable preference effect from heavier compression in commercial remasters ([JAES 2014](https://vbn.aau.dk/da/publications/perceptual-effects-of-dynamic-range-compression-in-popular-music-)). Untrained listeners could not detect up to 12 dB of limiting above chance ([Ronan et al., AES 2016](https://www.aes.org/e-lib/browse.cfm?elib=18076)). Mastering engineers only partly could, and audibility tracked the music's resulting crest factor rather than the amount removed ([Ronan et al., JAES 2017](https://www.aes.org/e-lib/browse.cfm?elib=19176)). Against that, Wendl and Lee found that both loudness and quality ratings changed significantly as crest factor fell from 15 to 9 dB, with the two correlated for rock ([AES 136](https://pure.hud.ac.uk/en/publications/the-effect-of-dynamic-range-compression-on-loudness-and-quality-p/)). Deruty and Tardieu showed the loudness war mainly damaged micro-dynamics and transients rather than section-level dynamics ([JAES 2014](https://aes2.org/publications/elibrary-page/?id=17085)). That is the snare-crack and cymbal-decay territory the client is complaining about. Level bias is also strong: in one AES study a 10 dB level difference dominated the "weak perceptual cues" of compression ([AES E-Library](https://www.aes.org/e-lib/browse.cfm?elib=17169)). Trade practice matches levels to within 0.1 dB because a slightly louder version is heard as "more detailed" rather than louder ([Stereophile](https://stereophile.com/content/mark-levinson-no38s-preamplifier-level-matching)). *Position:* the client's -9.5 LUFS preference is legitimate for personal listening, since nothing will turn it down. However, it was probably formed in unmatched comparisons. Render a -11 LUFS version with the same EQ, gain-match it to the -9.5 version, and let the client pick blind. If -9.5 still wins, keep it, and spend the limiting with 4x oversampling and the slowest release that avoids pumping. The **-1.2 dBTP ceiling is sound** for lossless playback. Drop to -2 dBTP for any AAC/MP3 copy made for a phone.

## A recommended chain for the Demucs pipeline

The chain below combines the sourced ordering rules (compressor before limiter, true-peak control last among processors, dither once at the end) with inferred placement for the separation-specific steps. It works entirely in 32-bit float until export.

| # | Stage | Settings | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Measure source | LUFS-I, LRA, LUFS-S max, dBTP, TT DR, sample histogram | Inference (DR method per [Stereophile](https://stereophile.com/content/dynamic-range-database); histogram per [RX docs](https://help.izotope.com/docs/rx-plug-in-pack/De-clip.html)) |
| 1 | Gain down in float | -1 to -2 dB | Removes the +0.2 dBTP inter-sample overs at zero risk; inference |
| 2 | Optional repair | De-clip only where flat-tops appear; de-limit (De-limiter / Unlimiter) at low Amount; A/B against no repair | Tools sourced ([iZotope](https://www.izotope.com/en/learn/repairing-a-distorted-audio-track); [arXiv 2308.01187](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.01187)); placement before separation is inference (limited input is out-of-domain for separators) |
| 3 | Separate | htdemucs_ft, several `--shifts`, float stems; residual = input minus sum of stems | Model/flags sourced ([Demucs README](https://github.com/facebookresearch/demucs)); residual handling inference |
| 4 | Per-stem corrective EQ | Moves from the table above; within plus or minus 3 dB, Q of 1.5 or lower | [Mastering The Mix](https://www.masteringthemix.com/blogs/learn/how-to-eq-during-mastering); [Wierstorf](https://aes2.org/publications/elibrary-page/?id=19277) |
| 5 | Per-stem dynamics | Vocal de-ess 5-8 kHz; drum transient shaping; guitar dynamic EQ 2.5-5 kHz; otherwise none | Partly sourced (de-ess, dynamic EQ); rest inference |
| 6 | Stem gains | Within plus or minus 2 dB (vocal +1 to +2 typical) | Inference from [Wierstorf](https://aes2.org/publications/elibrary-page/?id=19277), [Yang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.13634) |
| 7 | Sum stems + residual at unity | Null-test with stages 4-6 bypassed | Inference |
| 8 | Bus EQ | Broad tilt only, plus or minus 1-2 dB | "Balance, don't match" ([Shepherd, SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/mastering-essentials-eq-balance-dont-match)) |
| 9 | Bus compressor | 1.5:1-2:1, ~30-40 ms attack, ~100-150 ms or auto release, soft knee, 1-2 dB GR; no multiband | [Sage Audio](https://www.sageaudio.com/articles/mastering-compression-settings); [Katz](https://www.digido.com/?p=1509) |
| 10 | True-peak limiter | 4x oversampling, ceiling -1.2 dBTP (-2.0 for lossy copies), 1-3 dB GR | [FabFilter](https://www.fabfilter.com/help/pro-l/using/truepeaklimiting); [Katz](https://www.digido.com/?p=1509); [Spotify](https://support.spotify.com/us/artists/article/loudness-normalization/) |
| 11 | Export | 24-bit FLAC with flat TPDF dither, or 32-bit float with none; 16-bit only with noise-shaped dither, applied once | [iZotope](https://www.izotope.com/en/learn/what-is-dithering-in-audio.html) |

Verification matters as much as the settings. Loudness-match every comparison against the original CD by integrated LUFS, then check short-term loudness in the loudest section. BS.1770 has known low-frequency weighting errors, and listeners' by-ear balances deviated from equal-LUFS by over 4 LU in multitrack tests, so a bassier version can sound louder at the same LUFS ([Huddersfield](https://pure.hud.ac.uk/en/publications/alternative-weighting-filters-for-multi-track-program-loudness-me/)). Keep a fixed headphone volume, the headphone analogue of Katz's calibrated 83 dB SPL monitoring ([Digido](https://www.digido.com/portfolio-item/level-practices-part-2/)). Headphone preference tracks deviation from the Harman target (r = 0.91 for in-ears), so the client's headphones are themselves an EQ ([Olive et al., AES 143](https://www.aes.org/events/143/papers/?ID=5578)). Cross-check on one other system, or compare long-term spectra against the CD numerically. A mono fold-down check after any M/S width move is cheap insurance (inference).

## Conclusion

The research reframes the project's main lever. The client's complaints are masking problems, and stem separation now makes masking-reduction EQ possible on a finished master. That is the part worth investing in, within a roughly 2 to 3 dB envelope set by separation quality. Loudness is the part to economize on. The source already spent its peak headroom, and every extra dB of PLR taken out to reach -9.5 LUFS works against the cymbal and transient clarity the client is asking for. If the loudness-matched blind test confirms -9.5 LUFS, keep it knowingly. If it doesn't, the same chain at about -11 LUFS will likely sound more open without losing any perceived impact.

Two uncertainties remain open. No study found measures Demucs on hard-limited, distorted punk or metal masters, or evaluates the "process stems, add residual back" technique perceptually. And no source in scope gives sourced settings for de-reverbing a vocal stem, so the "barn" vocal is the move most likely to need ear-led iteration and to show artifacts first. Solo-checking the processed vocal stem for smeared tails, then judging it only in the full sum, is the practical safeguard.
