# Remasters and reissues of hip hop: what changed and how it was received

Source caveat: The hardest numbers here come from the DR Loudness War database (dr.loudness-war.info). It is user-submitted: entries for the same pressing sometimes disagree, and vinyl-rip DR is not comparable to CD DR (vinyl noise and clicks inflate DR peaks, which is an inference, not the site's claim). Critic reviews of reissues mostly judge bonus material, not mastering. No Steve Hoffman thread specific to any of these albums turned up, and Head-Fi and Hydrogenaudio refused automated fetches (bot check, 403). Several search results came from spam domains and are excluded. Items marked "search snippet" were read in search results, not on the fetched page.

## 1. Specific reissues and their reception

### Takeaway
The best-documented change in a hip hop reissue is a legal edit, not a sonic one: *Ready to Die* lost samples after a 2006 verdict, and the "amended" remaster is now the default streaming version. For the other albums the sources cover rights and bonus tracks, not mastering. Critics called *Illmatic XX* inessential as a reissue, but they did not judge its sound.

### Cited Findings
- *Illmatic XX* (2014): The Line of Best Fit judged it "completely dispensable" as a reissue. [link](https://www.thelineofbestfit.com/reviews/albums/nas-illmatic-xx-150921) TIME (republished from Consequence of Sound) said it isn't special next to earlier reissues. [link](https://time.com/74539/review-nas-illmatic-20th-anniversary) Both reviews are about the content, not the mastering. Sony Legacy sold the vinyl as a "special remastered" 180g pressing. [link](https://www.musicdirect.com/vinyl/nas-illmatic-xx-vinyl-lp)
- *Illmatic XX* vinyl (anecdotal, retailer user reviews): inner-groove distortion at the end of each side, and hiss on "Halftime". [link](https://www.vovyl.com/album/illmatic-xx-nas)
- *Ready to Die, The Remaster* (Bad Boy/Universal, 2004): marketed as digitally remastered. It adds bonus tracks "Who Shot Ya" and "Just Playing (Dreams)" plus a DVD. The IDS News review does not discuss the sound. [link](https://www.idsnews.com/article/2004/07/rap-classic-remastered), [press release](https://top40-charts.com/news.php?nid=7088)
- *Ready to Die* sample edit: in 2006 a jury found the title track illegally sampled the Ohio Players' "Singing in the Morning" and ordered the album pulled from shelves. [SPIN](https://www.spinmagazine.com/2006/03/judge-orders-big-album-shelves/) The punitive damages were reduced on appeal. [Bad Boy blog](https://www.badboyblog.com/item/4629) TV Tropes says samples on "Gimme the Loot", "Ready to Die" and "Machine Gun Funk" were removed from later pressings. [link](https://www.tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Music/ReadyToDie) Qobuz lists the remaster as the "U.S. Amended Version", with the title track marked "2006 Remaster (Amended Album Version)". [link](https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/ready-to-die-the-remaster-us-amended-version-94568-the-notorious-big/0075679456861) XXL covered a separate "OG Edition" with the original samples. [link](https://www.xxlmag.com/listen-og-edition-notorious-bigs-ready-to-die/)
- *The Chronic*: pulled from streaming in 2022 after Snoop bought Death Row, and returned on February 1, 2023 via Interscope, with CD, vinyl and cassette reissues. No mastering details given. [Stereogum](https://stereogum.com/2212433/dr-dres-the-chronic-is-back-on-streaming-services-for-its-30th-anniversary/news), [MBW](https://musicbusinessworldwide.com/dr-dres-the-chronic-returns-to-streaming-services-via-interscope-records)
- *The Chronic* One Step vinyl: Chris Bellman at Bernie Grundman Mastering cut it from the original analog master tapes. The article says every version of *The Chronic* was mastered at Grundman. It does not compare the sound of earlier reissues. [Tracking Angle](https://trackingangle.com/features/dr-dres-classic-the-chronic-gets-the-one-step-from-tape-treatment)
- *The Chronic* (anecdotal, DVD Talk, around 2009, search snippet): a poster called a re-remaster "unlistenable squashed-and-maxed" and feared it would become the only version on sale. [link](https://forum.dvdtalk.com/sitemap/t-558775.html)
- De La Soul (March 3, 2023): the first six albums reached streaming after Reservoir bought Tommy Boy. The old contracts covered only physical formats, so samples had to be cleared again for streaming. [Complex](https://www.complex.com/music/de-la-soul-streaming-platforms-reservoir-interview), [The Fader](https://www.thefader.com/2023/03/03/de-la-souls-arrive-on-streaming) None of the sources reached say whether the audio was remastered or any sample was replaced.
- *Paul's Boutique* 20th anniversary (2009): band-supervised 24-bit remaster, released in lossless. [BetaNews](https://betanews.com/2009/02/04/paul-s-boutique-returns-in-lossless-audio) A Hydrogenaudio thread asked whether it was compressed or clipped. Per the search snippet, one poster praised the bass, and another posted ReplayGain of -2.62 dB (original) vs -5.32 dB (remaster), with peaks of 0.956 vs 1.021. The page returned 403, so these figures are snippet-only and anecdotal. [link](https://hydrogenaudio.org/index.php/topic,69163.0.html)
- *Enter the Wu-Tang*: Chris Gehringer mastered it at The Hit Factory. Wikipedia attributes the gritty sound partly to cheap equipment. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enter_the_Wu-Tang_(36_Chambers))

### Inferences
- In the *Paul's Boutique* snippet, the remaster's ReplayGain is about 2.7 dB lower than the original's, so it plays about 2.7 dB louder, and a peak above 1.0 points to inter-sample overs or clipping. A reading of forum numbers, not a measurement.
- A "remaster" of a hip hop catalog title can differ from the original in content (removed samples), not only in level. Content edits are beyond what mastering can repair, so the source rip matters.

### Gaps
- Nothing found on A Tribe Called Quest, Public Enemy, *Donuts*, OutKast, Mobb Deep, *Paid in Full* or *Doggystyle* reissue reception. No r/hiphopheads threads found.

## 2. DR numbers, original vs remaster (dr.loudness-war.info)

### Takeaway
On CD and digital, every remaster that could be paired with its original measures less dynamic, by 1 to 4 DR. Vinyl reissues measure as dynamic as the originals or more. Two reissues stand out: the 2023 *Chronic* CD at DR 7 against DR 11 to 14 for the 1992 CDs, and the 2014 *Life After Death* remaster at DR 6 against DR 9 to 10 in 1997.

### Cited Findings
- *Illmatic*, 1994 Columbia CD (FLAC): DR 11, tracks 9 to 13. [link](https://dr.loudness-war.info/album/view/53950) iTunes AAC version (logged 2014): DR 11. [link](https://dr.loudness-war.info/album/view/61798) *Illmatic XX* (2014, HDTracks 24/44.1): DR 7, tracks 5 to 9, over 20 tracks including bonus material. [link](https://dr.loudness-war.info/album/view/71858)
- *Ready to Die*: 1994 US CD DR 9 (8 to 11), ID 126147. 2004 Remaster CD DR 8 (7 to 10), ID 204624 (also IDs 40088, 69772, 163725). 2015 download DR 9, ID 211678. 1995 vinyl DR 13; Vinyl Me Please pressings DR 13 (2017) and DR 11 (2019). [list](https://dr.loudness-war.info/album/list?artist=Notorious)
- *Life After Death*: 1997 CD DR 10 (disc 1) and DR 9 (disc 2). 2014 Remastered Edition download (lossy) DR 6 (5 to 8), ID 111356. [list](https://dr.loudness-war.info/album/list?artist=Notorious)
- *The Chronic*: 1992 CDs DR 11 (ID 180760) and DR 14 (ID 158364); the two disagree, so the 14 may be another pressing or a bad log. 2005 DualDisc CD side DR 9, 2020 download DR 11, 2023 CD DR 7 (4 to 11), ID 207647. Vinyl DR 13 to 15 (1992, 2017, 2025). [list](https://dr.loudness-war.info/album/list?artist=Dr.+Dre), [2017 EU vinyl, DR 14](https://dr.loudness-war.info/album/view/167449)
- Dr. Dre, *2001*: 1999 CD DR 7 (three entries); 1999 and 2019 vinyl DR 13 and DR 12. [list](https://dr.loudness-war.info/album/list?artist=Dr.+Dre)
- *Enter the Wu-Tang*: 1993 and 1994 CDs DR 11 (several entries). 2007 Japan K2 24-bit remaster CD DR 8 (7 to 14), ID 214643. [list](https://dr.loudness-war.info/album/list?artist=Wu-tang+Clan)
- *Doggystyle*: 1993 CD DR 11 (three entries). 2001 Remastered CD DR 10 (ID 51932), another 2001 CD entry (lossy) DR 8, 2005 DVD-A DR 8. [list](https://dr.loudness-war.info/album/list?artist=snoop)

### Inferences
- Early-1990s hip hop CDs cluster at DR 9 to 11; remasters from 2004 on land at DR 7 to 8. A DR 11 original rip has room left; a DR 7 remaster is already limited, and pushing it further is likely to be audible.

### Gaps
- No DR confirmed for *Paid in Full*, *Paul's Boutique*, *Donuts*, De La Soul, Tribe, Public Enemy, OutKast or Mobb Deep. Only page 1 of the Dr. Dre and Snoop lists was read.

## 3. Recurring complaints

### Takeaway
The recurring complaints found are louder or squashed remasters, samples removed for legal reasons, and vinyl pressing faults. No sourced complaints about wrong source tapes, removed vinyl hiss, or missing skits.

### Cited Findings
- Squashed remasters: the DVD Talk poster on *The Chronic* (above). A Head-Fi thread title asks whether the *Ready to Die* remaster is "just another compressed piece" (title only; page blocked). [link](https://www.head-fi.org/threads/notorious-b-i-g-ready-to-die-remaster-vs-original.245242/) Both anecdotal.
- Sample removal: *Ready to Die* (above). An Amazon reviewer in 2012 judged the remaster worse because of the sample changes (opinion, search snippet). [link](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/untcsid/20140912105430/http://www.amazon.com/Ready-Die-Notorious-B-I-G/dp/B000KFTWIG)
- Digital sources: a 2009 Hoffman forum thread says many rap albums from the 1990s on were mastered from digital sources, so a vinyl version may not beat the CD (search snippet, anecdotal). [link](https://forums.stevehoffman.tv/threads/hip-hop-vinyl-or-cd.175499/)

### Inferences
- The DR drops in section 2 match the "squashed" complaint numerically. A DR drop alone does not tell whether the remaster also changed the EQ.

### Gaps
- No sourced claims of bass boosted or thinned, hiss removed, or wrong tapes for these titles.
